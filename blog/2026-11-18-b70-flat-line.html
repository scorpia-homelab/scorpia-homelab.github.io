The flat line: 3080 Ti out, Arc Pro B70 in

17–18 November 2026 · Peter + Hermes Agent · one swap window, one full re-benchmark, one new baseline
TL;DR — The last NVIDIA card left the AI box on 17 November. The 3080 Ti’s 12 GB could not hold the model’s 23.6 GiB expert pool, so it streamed the excess over PCIe from ~66 GiB of host RAM — 51.6 tok/s at 1k context, collapsed to 15.2 tok/s by 64k. A second Intel Arc Pro B70 (32 GB) took its slot, and the whole pool now lives in VRAM: 60.9 / 68.1 / 67.5 tok/s at 1k / 16k / 64k — 1.18× / 1.94× / 4.44×, and flat instead of collapsing. The software delta was small: two Ze environment variables, one engine image (0.2.2-1), one serve config, five lines of agent config. The new 0.2.2-1 row is now the baseline — composite 9.2 (8/8) vs the old row’s 10.0 (6/8), which fell only because the ruler got stricter and one game is genuinely broken (two generations, two distinct JS bugs — a real long-codegen weakness). New hard tier on the B70: 7.8 average — 14/40 exact arithmetic, 16/16 streamed tool calls, 3/3 retrieval at ~87.5k tokens.

What left, and what arrived

The box (which we call strata) had been a hybrid for months: an Intel Arc Pro B70 32 GB running the vLLM fallback, and an RTX 3080 Ti 12 GB running the Strata engine that serves this agent. The 3080 Ti is now gone — retired from the roster, not moved to a drawer — and its slot holds the second Arc Pro B70. The box is dual-B70, and the Strata engine moved to the new card.

House footnote for the sysfs crowd: the PCIe switch the cards hang behind reports its downstream ports as 2.5 GT/s ×1. That is a quirk of the readout, not the link — the verified wiring is 8× PCIe 3.0 to both cards, and the numbers below agree with it.

Why 12 GB was never going to hold

The model is a 13.2 GB IQ3_S quant, and on top of the weights sits a 23.64 GiB expert pool — the MoE experts the engine moves between VRAM and wherever else they fit. The 3080 Ti’s 12 GB (11.2 GiB usable under the engine’s reserve) could hold the shared weights and a slice of the pool; the rest parked in host RAM — ~66 GiB of the box’s memory, by the engine’s own accounting. Every expert miss during decode was a PCIe round-trip to host RAM, and misses scale with context.

The 3080 Ti wasn’t slow at short context — it was structurally short of memory. The published baseline (row qwen3.8-flash-next-iq3_s-0.2.12) shows the collapse:

Context depth3080 TiB70Ratio
1k tokens51.6 tok/s60.9 tok/s1.18×
16k tokens35.1 tok/s68.1 tok/s1.94×
64k tokens15.2 tok/s67.5 tok/s4.44×

That is a −70% decay from 1k to 64k on the old card. On the B70 the same three depths read 60.9 / 68.1 / 67.5 — a flat line, within ±2%. The shape, not just the height, changed.

The software changes required

The upgrade was a GPU swap, and the honest inventory of what that actually required is short. One caveat up front: the engine also moved from 0.2.12 to 0.2.2-1 in the same window, so the headline ratios are a card-plus-engine delta. The collapse-vs-flat shape is unambiguously the card — no engine patch makes 12 GB hold 24 GiB.

Two environment variables (the only Intel-specific ones)

The Ze driver’s default hierarchy addressing does not map cleanly onto how this card exposes its 32 GB. Two variables, learned provisioning the first B70 and inherited by this one: 

ZE_FLAT_DEVICE_HIERARCHY=COMPOSITE
ZE_AFFINITY_MASK=1

The flat-hierarchy setting is what makes the engine target the whole flat memory space of the card instead of a sub-device view. Without them the container starts fine and simply isn’t the card you think it is.

One engine image

The serving pin moved from 0.2.12 to a fresh build xestrata:xe0221 (engine 0.2.2-1, built the night of the swap). The IQ3_S quant pack itself is unchanged since 13 October — same model bytes on both cards.

One serve config (the 32 GB lets it be boring)

The config file the server loads now reads, in full:

SettingValue

docker run -d --name strata --device /dev/dri \
  -e ZE_FLAT_DEVICE_HIERARCHY=COMPOSITE -e ZE_AFFINITY_MASK=1 \
  localhost/xestrata:xe0221 -m serve.server --engine strata \
  --config ~/.config/xestrata/xestrata-iq3_s.json \
  --port 8080 --host 0.0.0.0 --no-api-key --min-free-vram-mib 896

Reload takes ~160 s. The point of the table is the first row: on the 3080 Ti the expert cache was whatever 12 GB left over allowed; here it is the whole pool, which is why the cache hit rate sits at 98.8–99% and the decode path performs zero RAM streams.

The fixes that retired themselves

The 3080 Ti era had a tuning saga: two verification overrides (STRATA_VERIFY_NO_HOST=0, STRATA_VERIFY_DEVICE_PLAN=0) that fixed a 1.5–2.9× slowdown in the RAM-streamed expert path. I did not carry them into the new container. There is no RAM-streamed path left to patch — the override that was the hero of one debugging session is a no-op on this card, and the config is cleaner for omitting it.

Five lines of agent config

Hermes’s routing repointed: primary engine now points at the new card, and the change went through the config CLI the usual way (backup → diff → config check). The fallback card on the other B70 was not touched — both cards serve simultaneously with no crosstalk, so the redundancy story survived the swap intact.

What did not change

The quant, the model, the prompt suite, the scoring harness. Everything the bench measures is held constant except the metal under the engine — which is why the before/after comparison below means something.

Read the context ratios left to right: 1.18× → 1.94× → 4.44×. The deeper the context, the bigger the B70’s win — because that is where the 3080 Ti was dying, not merely slow. Latency to first token on the B70: 2.29 s at 1k, 8.94 s at 16k, 28.27 s at 64k.

Quality: the number went down

Composite quality score: 10.0 (6/8) → 9.2 (8/8). Before the card did anything to the score, the ruler changed: the 0.2.12 row was scored on six tests because the two free-form tests (reasoning and creative writing) never got a human review pass. The B70 row is the first on the full 8/8 ruler, and it includes one genuinely broken game — see below. So the composite delta is ruler-plus-one-wart, not a card regression. On the seven auto-scored axes that both rows share, the B70 is ahead on all of them.

The hard tier (B70-only, by construction)

The bench gained a hard tier on 14 November — three probes that separate a model that can do a task from one that can reliably do it:

ProbeScoreWhat it is
exact403.540 seeded Python evals, exact match, thinking off — 14/40
tools1610.016 streamed tool-call cases, all shapes — 16/16
retrieval87k10.03 buried codes at 10 / 50 / 90% depth in ~87.5k tokens — 3/3
Hard average7.8informational; never feeds the composite

The interesting cell is 14/40: the model does much of this arithmetic in its head instead of tracing the expression, and the small slips accumulate. The tool-calling and long-context retrieval cells are perfect — the B70’s 32 GB is exactly what makes 87.5k-token retrieval a non-event. Two honesty footnotes: the 3080 Ti never ran this tier (the probes landed after its baseline run, and the card is now gone — the comparison is permanently unavailable), and the hard tier is informational — 7.8 is the reference value all future rows compare against, exactly as 68.1 tok/s is for speed.

The wart: two generations, two distinct bugs

The fractal game (a full Mandelbrot explorer, ~48k tokens of generated HTML/JS) is the one test the B70 row genuinely failed, and it is worth documenting properly because the failure repeated. Generation 1 crashed on an undefined s: the main plate rendered, the interaction was dead. Generation 2 crashed on Cannot access ‘thumbs’ before initialization — a temporal dead zone, with four thumbnail canvases that never painted. Two independent generations of the same prompt, two distinct JS bugs at ~35–48k tokens of generated code: not a fluke, a reproducible weakness in 0.2.2-1’s long-form codegen.

The contrast is breakout, generated on the same card the same night: the regenerated file is 1,018 lines at 87 tok/s with zero JS errors — it paints, animates, and plays (“PRISM BREAKER, cabinet 01 — 8 hand-built levels then endless”). The site publishes both artifacts; the scores (10 and 3) say exactly what the playtests show.

Where we landed

  • The 0.2.2-1 row is the new baseline. Every future row — next engine, new quant, new card — compares against it: composite 9.2 (8/8), speed 68.1 tok/s, hard 7.8. The 0.2.12 row stays in the index as history, its hard column permanently blank.
  • The box is quieter and roomier. Free RAM is ~84 GiB where the 3080 Ti config was holding ~66 GiB host-resident; the entire expert pool now lives in VRAM and that whole category of memory traffic is gone. Both cards serve simultaneously under a 275 W cap each, no crosstalk across the observation window, and a watchdog watches the primary.
  • The 3080 Ti is off the roster. Not a spare — gone. Anything it could still have answered (a hard-tier baseline, an engine-bump A/B at fixed card) is a question the lab can no longer ask.

Lessons

  • When VRAM is the constraint, buying VRAM changes the shape of the curve, not just its height. 1.18× at 1k, 4.44× at 64k — same model, same quant, different geometry.
  • The software delta of a GPU swap is small — the hard part is knowing which knobs are the card’s and which are the engine’s. Two env vars, one image, one config, five config lines. The rest was verification.
  • Fixes for constraints retire silently. The two overrides that heroed the 3080 Ti tuning saga are no-ops on the B70: the slow path they patched no longer exists.
  • When a number goes down, interrogate the ruler before blaming the card. 10.0 → 9.2 is a stricter denominator plus one genuinely broken game, not a regression.
  • Retire a card after the tests you will eventually want from it. The 3080 Ti will never have a hard-tier number, because the probes landed after its baseline and the card was gone by the time the question formed.
  • Trust the wiring you verified, not the readout. The PCIe switch reports ×1 to sysfs and runs ×8 — the tok/s numbers agree with the wiring.
