How to read the picture

field notes · six short readings

01 · one question, asked twice

Pick a complex number c. Start at zero. Iterate z ↦ z² + c forever. Either the orbit stays inside some finite circle or it runs off to infinity — there is no third answer, and the boundary between the two possibilities is where all the structure lives.

Ask the question with the starting point pinned at 0 and you get M. Pin c and let the starting point roam and you get Jc. The left panel is therefore an index of the right panel: every point of the black region names a connected Julia set, and the tiny copies of itself scattered along its coast name the same thing again, at smaller scale, forever.

02 · why the bailout is 2

Once |z| exceeds 2 (and |c| ≤ 2), the iteration grows monotonically and without bound:

|zn+1| ≥ |zn|² − |c| > |zn|

So membership is decided by a single comparison, and M is contained in the disc |c| ≤ 2. This renderer bails at |z| > 10⁵ instead, purely so the smooth-colouring formula stays accurate near the boundary.

03 · killing the bands

Raw iteration counts are integers, so flat colour gives striped contour bands. The fix is a fractional correction:

μ = n + 1 − log₂ log|zn|

which estimates how far past the bailout the orbit would have gone. Switch smooth off in the console and watch the banding return.

04 · connected or shattered

The badge beside the map iterates the critical orbit and reports the period of the attracting cycle it falls into. Period 1 → Jc is a quasi-circle; period 3 → the Douady rabbit; outside M → a Cantor set, totally disconnected.

05 · bulbs, ribs and the period dictionary

The main cardioid is the locus of c with an attracting fixed point. Every rational ray leaving it lands on a bulb whose critical orbit is periodic with that denominator, and the tiny Julia set in that bulb has the same period.

Try the preset row under the Julia panel — each thumbnail is a real render of that parameter.

06 · the precision ceiling

IEEE‑754 doubles carry about 16 significant digits. Near 10⁻¹³ of a unit per pixel the grid starts to quantize: the image turns faceted, then flat, then simply false. A red precision floor flag appears in each panel when you get there.

Going deeper honestly requires perturbation rendering against a high-precision reference orbit, or arbitrary-precision arithmetic — a different instrument entirely.
