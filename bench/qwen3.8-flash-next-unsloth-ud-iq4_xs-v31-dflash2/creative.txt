Station 7 had one rule: never open the airlock. For forty years, Mara polished the porthole, listening to Earth’s last broadcast loop—children laughing, birds, rain. She missed wind. When the station began dying, oxygen thinning, she finally suited up. Her hands shook as she cycled the airlock, expecting void. The outer door hissed open. Sunlight. A meadow rolled to a blue horizon. Behind her, the station’s speaker crackled: “Simulation complete. Welcome to Mars.” Mara looked down. Her boots pressed real grass. She had been born underground. The Earth broadcast had been a lullaby. Now, breathing fresh air, she finally wept.
