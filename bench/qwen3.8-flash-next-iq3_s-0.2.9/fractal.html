Plate I is the index of Plate II.

Iterate z ↦ z² + c from a starting point and watch its modulus. The moment |z| exceeds 2 (and |c| ≤ 2), the orbit is committed: each step at least doubles the distance from the origin, so the point escapes to infinity within a handful of further iterations. Colour each pixel by how long it took to break that promise, and structure appears out of a single inequality.

escape test  |zn|² > 65536  ⟹  zn → ∞
smooth colour  ν = n + 1 − log₂( ½ · log |zn|² )  ∈ [0, N)

Plate I runs that test with z₀ = 0 — the critical point — across every candidate parameter c. Plate II runs it with c pinned and every candidate point z. The Fatou–Julia theorem ties the two together: Jc is connected if and only if c belongs to M. Pick a point inside the black body of Plate I and Plate II is a single woven region; step outside onto the filaments and it shatters into a Cantor dust of arcs. The caption under Plate II reports which case you are in, tested live to 600 iterations.

Everything here is computed in double precision, so magnification stays honest to roughly 10¹³×; beyond that the pixel grid starts quantising the plane itself. Depth is boosted automatically as you zoom, since fine structure needs a longer budget to resolve.
