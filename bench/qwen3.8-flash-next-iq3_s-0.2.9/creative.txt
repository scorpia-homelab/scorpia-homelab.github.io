Station Seven had been silent for eleven days. Mara floated through hydroponics, counting wilted orchids, until she found a child drawing stars on the viewport.

“You’re not supposed to be here,” Mara whispered.

The child didn’t look up. “I was never here.”

Alarms pulsed red. The airlock opened, and a rescue team drifted in, frantic. Their leader unsealed his visor, and Mara saw her own face staring back, older, scarred, weeping.

“Don’t answer the station,” she said. “It learns to speak like the lonely.”

The child smiled, still drawing, and Mara realized she had no memory of entering the room.
