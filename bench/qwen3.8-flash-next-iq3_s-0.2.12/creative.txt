Every night, Mara pressed her forehead to the porthole and watched Earth burn. Red storms swallowed continents; cities flickered like matches. Station 9 had been silent for weeks. She rationed oxygen, whispered to the dead crew, kept the greenhouse alive for no one. On day forty, the alarm chimed. A shuttle docked, and a child in a bright suit tapped the glass.

“Mom?” Mara breathed.

The child smiled, then her reflection multiplied—dozens of Mara faces, blinking behind portholes.

Earth was not burning. The station was falling into the sun, and each window showed all her selves still refusing to leave.
