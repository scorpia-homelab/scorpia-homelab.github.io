The station’s only lighthouse keeper, Mara, polished the lens each shift, warning ships away from the dead moon. For years she radioed Earth: alone, faithful, safe. Then a supply drone delivered a letter: “Evacuation completed 2041. Why are you still there?” Mara frowned, checked the airlock logs, and found her own exit record, dated six years ago. Outside, the moon blinked—not rock, but an eye. The lens wasn’t warning ships away. It was keeping something asleep. Mara touched the glass, realizing her reflection had no face. The station hummed in the silence. She had just polished it awake.
